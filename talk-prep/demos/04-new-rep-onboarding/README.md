# Demo 4: New Rep Starting Monday

## The Shift
- **Mode 1 (Tool):** "Write an onboarding checklist for a new sales rep"
- **Mode 2 (Partner):** "Here's our product lines, our top 20 accounts with real context, our competitive landscape, and our pricing philosophy. Build a real onboarding playbook that transfers institutional knowledge — not just a task list."

## The Dataset
- `product-lines.md` — Complete guide to Hartland's product lines, brands, competitive positioning, cross-sell opportunities, pricing philosophy, and competitor cheat sheet
- `top-accounts.md` — Top 20 accounts with contact info, what they buy, relationship dynamics, opportunities, risks, and current red flags

## What Makes This Demo Work

### Mode 1 Output (generic)
A checklist like:
- [ ] Set up email and CRM access
- [ ] Review product catalog
- [ ] Shadow senior rep for first week
- [ ] Introduce to key accounts
- [ ] Review pricing policies

Technically correct. Completely useless for making someone productive.

### Mode 2 Output (with real context)
A playbook that includes things like:
- "Your first week: ride along with Jake Finley on the Great Plains Manufacturing visit. Tony Reeves (the plant manager) is a loyal customer who values service over price. Bring donuts for the shop floor. Watch how Jake talks to him — it's a masterclass in relationship-based selling."
- "Account to avoid until you're ready: Spirit AeroSystems. Aerospace quality requirements, zero tolerance for errors. Linda Choi (procurement) will eat you alive if you don't know your stuff. Let Jake handle Spirit for your first 6 months."
- "The Amazon Business playbook: When a customer says 'I can get that on Amazon for cheaper,' don't argue price. Say 'You're right — for commodity bearings, Amazon is fast and cheap. But when your line goes down at 2 AM and you need someone who knows whether a 6205-2RS or 6205-ZZ is the right replacement for a washdown environment, that's when you call us. We're not competing with Amazon — we're the people Amazon can't replace.'"
- "Cross-sell trigger: When you see a coupling order, ask about the bearings behind it. Couplings fail when shafts are misaligned, and misalignment kills bearings. A coupling sale should always lead to a bearing conversation."

### The Moment
The audience realizes that this isn't a generic onboarding template — it contains the actual tribal knowledge of their best reps, their real account dynamics, and specific coaching that would take months to absorb through osmosis. And it was generated in 2 minutes from documents they already have (or could write in a few hours).

## Demo Script
1. "Your best rep is retiring next year. How much of their knowledge is written down?" (pause — everyone knows the answer is 'almost none')
2. **Mode 1:** "Write an onboarding checklist for a new industrial sales rep." → Generic list.
3. **Mode 2:** Upload both docs. "Using our product lines and account intelligence, create an onboarding playbook for Tyler Novak, a new outside sales rep starting Monday. He has 3 years of industrial sales experience but is new to our territory and product lines. Include: week-by-week plan for first month, which accounts to prioritize and which to avoid, key relationships to build, product knowledge priorities, and specific coaching based on our competitive landscape."
4. **The moment:** The playbook mentions specific customers by name, flags the current red flags (Great Lakes Stamping is angry, Jensen Manufacturing owes money, Dairyland might need emergency service), and coaches the new rep on the personality and preferences of each contact. "This is what 20 years of institutional knowledge looks like when you finally write it down."
