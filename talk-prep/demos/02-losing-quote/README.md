# Demo 2: The Quote We're Losing

## The Shift
- **Mode 1 (Tool):** "Write a follow-up email to a customer who hasn't responded to our quote"
- **Mode 2 (Partner):** "Here's everything I know about this account, this deal, and the competitive situation. Help me figure out what's actually going on and how to approach it."

## The Dataset
`account-brief.md` — A detailed brief on Midwest Mechanical Services and a $42K valve package quote that's gone cold. Includes account history, competitive intel, relationship dynamics, and a same-day coupling emergency that complicates things.

## Key Insights AI Should Surface

1. **It's probably not about price.** Ferguson is typically more expensive. The silence + the Bray rep's intel points to **lead time** — Ferguson may have stock in Wichita vs. our 10-week drop-ship. For a contractor with a project timeline, 10 weeks vs. 4-6 weeks is the whole ballgame.

2. **The coupling emergency is actually an opportunity.** If Hartland finds a way to solve Tom's Friday deadline — even creatively (borrow from another PO, source from a different distributor, overnight from Rexnord) — it demonstrates exactly the kind of hustle that counters the lead time concern on the valve deal.

3. **Tom isn't the decision-maker.** His uncle Steve (the owner) signs big POs and is price-sensitive. Tom went to bat for Hartland on valves, which means Tom needs Hartland to help him make the case to Steve. The approach should arm Tom with ammunition, not just convince Tom.

4. **A phased delivery option could change the game.** Instead of 10 weeks for the full package: ship the ball valves and gate valves (in stock or short lead) within 2 weeks to get the project started, with the butterfly valves following on the Bray timeline. This removes the "wait 10 weeks to start" problem.

5. **Don't lead with discount.** The instinct is to cut price. But if the problem is lead time, a discount doesn't help. And if Steve is comparing a $42K quote to a $44K quote that ships in half the time, he'll take the $44K quote every time.

## Demo Script
1. **Mode 1:** "Write a follow-up email for a quote that's gone cold." → Generic "just checking in" email. Polite, forgettable, doesn't address the real problem.
2. **Mode 2:** Paste the full account brief. Ask: "Based on everything here, what do you think is really going on? What's my best approach — and what should I absolutely NOT do?"
3. **The conversation:** AI identifies the lead time theory, suggests the phased delivery approach, flags that the coupling emergency needs to be resolved first (or simultaneously) as a trust-builder, and recommends calling Tom (not emailing) with a specific plan rather than an open-ended "what can we do?"
4. **The moment:** The audience sees that AI didn't just write a better email — it changed the entire strategy. The rep was going to send a "checking in" email and maybe offer a discount. Now they're calling with a phased delivery proposal and a plan to solve the coupling emergency same-day.
