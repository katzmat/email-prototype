## 1. TALK THESIS & ARC

**Core argument:** Most people use AI at one or two levels. There are five. Each level is a simple, learnable shift — and the jump from level 2 to level 3 is where the real unlock starts.

**The Five Levels:**

1. **Question & Answer** — AI as a smarter search engine. "What is ER collet runout?" *Where everyone starts.*
2. **Task Execution** — AI does a single task. "Write a follow-up email to this distributor." *Where most people plateau.*
3. **Contextual Task Execution** — Same task, but you give it your world. "Given our collection policies, this account's history, and their $400K annual volume, draft a follow-up that's firm but preserves the relationship." *The input quality leap — same task, dramatically better output.*
4. **Thought Partner** — You're not asking it to *do* something, you're asking it to *think with you*. "I'm deciding between three pricing strategies for Q2. Here are the tradeoffs I see — push back on my assumptions." *The most underused and highest-value level for knowledge workers. Needs zero data, zero setup.*
5. **Workflow Engine** — Data + context + outcome = complete deliverable. "Here's my aging report, my collection policies, and my account notes. Produce a prioritized collections strategy with customized communications for each tier." *The multiplier.*

**Emotional arc:**
- Open with recognition: "You already use AI. You're good at it. Here's why that's the perfect foundation."
- Name the levels: "Most of you are great at levels 1 and 2. Today we're going to climb to 5." (5 min)
- Prove it with their world: 6 worked examples that walk up the levels (25-30 min)
- Close with adoption path: "Here's how to start Monday morning" (5 min)
- Q&A (15+ min)

## 2. TALK STRUCTURE (45 min + Q&A)

### Part 1: The Five Levels (8 min)

Show the spectrum on one slide. Don't belabor the framework — let the examples teach it.

#### Levels 1-2: Where You Are (1 min)
- Quick acknowledgment: you're already here, this is real skill, and it's the foundation
- The gap: most people get stuck because they don't know what's above this

#### Level 3: The Context Leap (3 min)
- **The problem:** AI gives generic answers because it knows nothing about your business
- **The solution: Context Documents** — Structured reference files you maintain and bring to every conversation
  - Company context (who we are, what we sell, our market position)
  - Role context (my responsibilities, KPIs, current priorities)
  - Project/account context (specific situations with their data and constraints)
- **Key insight:** You write these once, update occasionally, and reference them in every prompt. It's like giving a new hire a briefing packet instead of expecting them to figure everything out from scratch.
- **The compound effect:** When one person writes a great context doc, the whole team benefits. A senior rep's product knowledge captured in a doc makes every junior rep as knowledgeable as them.

#### Level 4: Thought Partner (2 min)
- **The shift:** You stop asking AI to *produce things* and start asking it to *think with you*
- **What it looks like:**
  - "I'm presenting Q1 results tomorrow. Here's my deck outline. What questions will leadership ask? What's the weakest part of my argument?"
  - "We're choosing between three vendors. Here are my evaluation criteria. What am I weighting wrong?"
  - "I need to have a difficult conversation with a distributor about missed SLAs. Help me plan the conversation — what's their likely perspective?"
- **Key insight:** This needs NO data uploads, NO tool access, NO setup. You can do this today in any AI chat. It's the highest-value level most people skip entirely.

#### Level 5: Workflow Engine (2 min)
- **What changes with data + tools:** AI can read your spreadsheets, chain reasoning steps, and produce complete deliverables
- **The outcome prompt:** Instead of describing a task, you describe the end state:
  - **Role**: Who the AI should think as
  - **Goal**: The desired end state (not the task)
  - **Constraints**: Budget, timeline, compliance rules
  - **Success criteria**: How you'd judge if the output is excellent
- **Safety note:** You always review outputs. AI proposes, you approve. The value is in the 90% of grunt work it handles so you can focus on the 10% that needs your judgment.

### Part 2: Six Worked Examples (27 min)

*The examples are ordered to climb the levels. The first two need ZERO setup — anyone can do them today. The middle one bridges to data. The last three show the full workflow engine.*

---

#### Example 1: Thought Partner — Presentation Prep & Strategy Pressure-Test (4 min)
*Level 4. Needs NOTHING — no data, no context docs, no tools. Anyone can do this today.*

- **Persona:** Any manager preparing for a leadership presentation or strategic decision.
- **Situation:** You're presenting Q1 sales results to the executive team tomorrow. Revenue is up 8% but two major accounts are showing declining order frequency. You know leadership will push on the account health story.
- **Before (Level 2):** "Help me write a slide about Q1 sales results." Gets a generic template.
- **After (Level 4):**
  - **No context doc needed. No data upload. Just conversation.**
  - "I'm presenting Q1 results to our executive team tomorrow. Revenue is up 8% YoY, but our #2 and #5 accounts by volume have each declined 15%+ in order frequency over the past two quarters. I think it's product mix shift — they're moving to competitor holders for high-speed aluminum work where we're weaker. Leadership tends to fixate on big-account risks. Help me: (1) stress-test my product-mix theory — what else could explain declining order frequency? (2) anticipate the three toughest questions I'll get, (3) plan how to frame the account declines as an actionable opportunity rather than a risk story."
  - **The conversation continues:** AI pushes back — "Have you checked whether their total toolholding spend is down or just shifting to competitors?" You say "I don't know." AI suggests: "That's the first question leadership will ask. Can you get that data before tomorrow?" You realize you can pull it from your distributor portal.
  - **Result:** You walk in prepared for every tough question, with a reframed narrative and a clear action plan. 20 minutes of AI conversation replaced 2 hours of anxious slide-tweaking.
- **The point:** This is the most underused level. You didn't upload anything. You didn't write a prompt template. You just *thought out loud with a smart partner.*

#### Example 2: Thought Partner — Navigating a Difficult Conversation (4 min)
*Level 4. Needs NOTHING. Immediately applicable to everyone.*

- **Persona:** Any people manager, account manager, or team lead.
- **Situation:** A key distributor has missed their quarterly purchase commitment for the second time. You need to have a direct conversation about it, but this is a 15-year relationship and they're still a top-10 account. You can't afford to lose them, but you can't keep letting commitments slide.
- **Before (Level 2):** "Write an email about a distributor missing their purchase commitment." Gets a stiff, corporate-sounding template that could damage the relationship.
- **After (Level 4):**
  - "I need to have a tough conversation with a distributor. Here's the situation: [explains context, history, relationship dynamics, what's at stake on both sides]. Help me: (1) see this from their perspective — what's likely going on in their business that's causing this? (2) plan the conversation — what's my opening, what tone do I set, what's my ask vs. my fallback position? (3) anticipate how they might respond and how I should handle each scenario. I want to be direct about the commitment issue while showing I value the relationship."
  - **The conversation evolves:** AI might say "Before you go hard on commitment, have you asked what's changed on their end? If they're losing share to a competitor in their market, pressuring on volume could push them to a cheaper toolholding supplier." You realize you should lead with curiosity, not confrontation.
  - **Result:** A thought-through conversation plan with contingencies. You've rehearsed the hard parts. Your instinct was to send a stern email; now you're going in with a discovery-first approach that's more likely to preserve the relationship AND solve the problem.
- **The point:** AI didn't write your email. It changed how you're approaching the entire situation. That's more valuable than any draft.

#### Example 3: Contextual Task Execution — Technical Support with a Context Doc (5 min)
*Level 3. Needs one context doc (the product selection guide). No sensitive data.*

- **Persona:** David, Technical Customer Service Rep. Handles inbound inquiries from machinists and purchasing managers selecting toolholding systems.
- **Situation:** Gets 30+ inquiries/day asking "which collet/holder do I need for X application?" Currently relies on personal knowledge and flipping through catalogs.
- **Before (Level 2):** "What Rego-Fix product should I recommend for high-speed aluminum milling?" Gets a vague answer that might be wrong.
- **After (Level 3):**
  - **Context doc:** Product selection guide with decision trees (material → operation → speed/feed range → holder type → specific SKU), plus common gotchas and upsell paths
  - **Prompt:** "You are a technical toolholding specialist for Rego-Fix. Use the product selection guide I've provided. A customer asks: 'We're running 6061 aluminum on a BT40 VMC at 18,000 RPM with 1/2" endmills. We're getting chatter at full depth. Currently using ER32 collets.' Provide: (1) likely root cause of the chatter, (2) primary product recommendation with specific SKU and why it's better for this application, (3) an alternative if budget is a concern, (4) compatibility notes for their BT40 spindle, (5) a draft response email that's technical but accessible."
  - **Result:** Expert-level recommendations in seconds instead of 15 minutes of catalog searching. New reps productive in days instead of months.
  - **The team multiplier:** David's 20 years of product knowledge is now captured in the context doc. Every rep on the team benefits. When David retires, that knowledge doesn't walk out the door.
- **The point:** One context doc transformed generic AI into a domain expert. This is the bridge — easy to build, massive payoff.

#### Example 4: Workflow Engine — Competitive Positioning Analysis (4 min)
*Level 5. Needs a context doc + data (public product specs). No sensitive data.*

- **Persona:** Sarah, Marketing Manager. Responsible for campaigns, trade show materials, and competitive positioning.
- **Situation:** Preparing materials for IMTS 2026. Needs a competitive comparison positioning powRgrip against Schunk TENDO, Haimer shrink-fit, and MST collet systems.
- **Before (Level 2):** "Write a comparison of powRgrip vs competitors." Gets generic fluff with potentially wrong specs.
- **After (Level 5):**
  - **Context doc:** Company positioning doc with Rego-Fix's key differentiators, brand voice guidelines, and target customer profiles (job shop owners, aerospace, medical device)
  - **Data:** Spreadsheet of published product specs (TIR, clamping force, tool change time, price points) from public catalogs
  - **Outcome prompt:** "You are a technical marketing strategist for precision toolholding. Using the competitive spec data and our brand positioning guide, create: (1) a technical comparison matrix for our sales team, (2) a customer-facing one-pager emphasizing TCO advantages, (3) three targeted email sequences for each customer segment. Success criteria: every claim must be traceable to a spec in the data, messaging aligns with our brand voice, TCO argument includes tool life and changeover time savings."
  - **Result:** A complete campaign package that would normally take 2-3 weeks of research and iteration, grounded in real specs.
- **The point:** This is level 5 — outcome prompt + context + data = complete deliverable.

#### Example 5: Workflow Engine — Collections Prioritization (5 min)
*Level 5. Needs context doc + financial data (fictional).*

- **Persona:** Mike, AR/Finance Analyst. Manages collections for ~150 active distributor accounts.
- **Situation:** End of Q1, $2.1M outstanding across accounts with different payment histories, order volumes, and relationship importance.
- **Before (Level 2):** "Help me write a collections email." Gets one generic template.
- **After (Level 5):**
  - **Context doc:** Role context with collection policies, escalation thresholds, and account notes (e.g., "Precision Tool Supply is our #2 account — white-glove treatment")
  - **Data:** AR aging report (CSV) with ~150 accounts: names, invoice numbers, amounts, days outstanding, payment history scores, annual volume, last contact
  - **Outcome prompt:** "You are a collections strategist for a B2B precision manufacturing supplier. Analyze the AR aging report against our collection policies and account context. Produce: (1) a prioritized action list ranked by expected cash recovery × collection probability, (2) draft communications for each tier — gentle reminder, firm follow-up, escalation — customized per account using their history and relationship status, (3) a summary for my manager showing total exposure by risk tier and recommended write-off candidates. Constraint: accounts over $100K annual volume get personalized language, not templates."
  - **Result:** Complete collections strategy with 40+ customized communications, risk analysis, and management reporting — 2-3 days of work compressed to 30 minutes of review.
- **The point:** This is the full multiplier. Data + context + outcome framing = work product that used to take days.

#### Example 6: Workflow Engine — Win/Loss Analysis (5 min)
*Level 5. Needs context doc + sales data (fictional).*

- **Persona:** James, Sales Operations Analyst. Supports quoting, pipeline reporting, and win/loss analysis.
- **Situation:** Quoted $4.2M last quarter, closed $1.8M. Leadership wants to know why and what to change.
- **Before (Level 2):** "What are common reasons for low win rates in manufacturing sales?" Gets generic industry advice unconnected to their reality.
- **After (Level 5):**
  - **Context doc:** Company context with product lines, typical deal sizes, sales stages, and strategic priorities
  - **Data:** Quote log (CSV) with 200+ quotes: customer, product mix, discount level, deal size, competitor mentioned, sales rep, region, days-to-close, win/loss
  - **Outcome prompt:** "You are a sales analytics consultant for a precision toolholding manufacturer. Analyze the quote log to find statistically meaningful patterns. Produce: (1) win rate analysis segmented by product line, deal size, discount tier, region, and rep, (2) identification of 'sweet spot' deals — highest win rate × margin, (3) quotes we likely over-discounted — won deals where win probability was already high, (4) recommended pricing guardrails based on the patterns, (5) a 'deals we should have won' analysis for losses over $25K. Specific numbers, not generalities."
  - **Result:** Data-driven sales strategy that would normally require a BI analyst and 2 weeks.
- **The point:** Same pattern — context + data + outcome framing. Once you learn the pattern, you can apply it to any data-to-decision workflow.

### Part 3: Your Monday Morning Playbook (5 min)

#### Today: Start Thinking Out Loud (Level 4 — right now)
- Next time you're preparing for a meeting, making a decision, or planning a difficult conversation — open an AI chat and think through it together
- No setup required. No context docs. No data uploads. Just conversation.
- Try it once this week. You'll be surprised how much it changes your preparation.

#### This Week: Write Your First Context Doc (Level 3 — 30 minutes)
- Spend 30 minutes writing a "My Role" context doc:
  - What I'm responsible for
  - What I'm measured on
  - My current top 3 priorities
  - Key constraints (budget, timeline, compliance)
  - Tools/systems I use daily
- Start every AI conversation by referencing it: "Using the context in [doc], help me..."
- **This is the single highest-ROI thing you can do.** 30 minutes of writing transforms every future AI interaction.

#### This Month: Identify Your First Workflow (Level 5)
- Find a task you do repeatedly that involves: data → analysis → output
- Map the steps you do manually
- Write an outcome prompt that describes the end state
- Test it, refine it, save the prompt as a template

#### The Team Multiplier: Share What Works (Ongoing)
- When you write a context doc that works, share it with your team
- When you craft a prompt that produces great output, save it as a template
- When a senior person captures their expertise in a context doc, the whole team levels up
- **One person's context doc can make every new hire productive in days instead of months**

**Consider a live moment:** Take a real question or situation from the audience and work through it together, climbing the levels in real time. More memorable than any pre-built example.

## 4. DATA CREATION APPROACH

For each example, the data will be:
- **Realistic in structure and scale** — Real column headers, realistic distributions, plausible names/numbers
- **Fictional in content** — No actual Rego-Fix customer data, made-up account names and figures
- **Internally consistent** — Numbers add up, dates make sense, patterns exist to be found
- **Analytically interesting** — The data contains actual patterns that the AI can surface (not random noise)

For Examples 3 and 4 (no sensitive data needed), product specs and catalog data will be based on publicly available information from Rego-Fix's published catalogs and competitor public marketing materials.

Examples 1 and 2 need no data at all — they're pure conversation.

## 6. KEY MESSAGING NOTES

### What to AVOID
- Don't say "AI will replace jobs" — say "AI will replace *tasks* so you can focus on judgment"
- Don't show overly technical setups — keep it to copy-paste accessible
- Don't promise perfection — always frame as "AI drafts, you refine"
- Don't use Silicon Valley jargon — "agentic" becomes "multi-step AI workflows"
- Don't frame everything as needing data and prep — lead with what needs NOTHING

### What to EMPHASIZE
- **The first prompt doesn't need to be perfect.** AI is a conversation, not a one-shot exam. You iterate, you push back, you refine together.
- **Thought partnering is free and immediate.** No setup, no data, no tools. Just open a chat and think out loud. Most people skip this level entirely and it's the most valuable one.
- **Context docs are the single highest-ROI investment.** 30 minutes of writing transforms every future interaction.
- **The team multiplier.** One person's context doc makes the whole team smarter. One person's prompt template saves everyone time. Knowledge compounds.
- **They already have the hardest skill** (prompting). The five levels build on what they know.
- **The examples use data they already have.** No new tools needed to start.
- Rego-Fix's business is built on precision — applying that same precision to how they use AI is a natural fit.

### Key reframe from old version
The old plan had "three shifts" (outcome prompts, context docs, agentic workflows) as the framework. The new framework is **five levels of AI use** — this is more intuitive, more gradual, and critically it includes **thought partnering** (the most valuable and most overlooked level) and starts from where people actually are today. The three shifts are still present — they're the *mechanisms* that move you between levels — but the levels are the organizing principle, not the shifts.
